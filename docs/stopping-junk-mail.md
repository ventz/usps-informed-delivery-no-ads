# How to stop physical junk mail

*Last verified: 2026-08-16. Every URL here was fetched and read on that date.*

Almost all of this is **done once**. An afternoon now, then roughly half an hour
every five years. Work the steps in order — they're ranked by how much mail each
removes per minute spent.

Print this page and write your dates in the blanks; the expiries are the part
everyone forgets.

---

## The steps

### 1. Prescreened credit & insurance offers — free, ~5 min
**<https://www.optoutprescreen.com>** or **1-888-5-OPT-OUT** (1-888-567-8688)

Required by federal law (FCRA §604(e)) and binds all four bureaus — Equifax,
Experian, TransUnion **and Innovis**. The biggest single win, and the offers it
kills are the ones most worth killing.

- Online = **5 years**. Permanent starts online but you must **sign and mail back**
  the form it gives you. Do the permanent version.
- It asks for SSN and date of birth. **Both are optional** — per the FTC they only
  improve matching.
- This is also the opt-out that is legally printed on the offers themselves: every
  prescreened solicitation must carry a "PRESCREEN & OPT-OUT NOTICE" with that
  toll-free number (CFPB Reg V, 12 CFR §1022.54).

### 2. Broad prospect mail — $8, ~10 min, 10 years
**<https://www.dmachoice.org>** ($9 if you pay by mail)

Run by the advertising industry's own trade association, which estimates ~80%
reduction. Allow **90 days**.

Read what it does *not* cover before paying — it excludes unaddressed mail, small
local retailers, political mail, and **anyone you've bought from, subscribed to or
donated to in the past two years**.

`Done: __________  Renew by: __________ (10 years)`

### 3. The shared-mail envelopes — free, ~10 min
This is where the physical bulk is.

| Mailer | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Clipp / Local Flavor | <https://www.clipp.com/remove-address> | A real self-serve address form — the only clean one left. Takes several weeks. The widely-cited `clippermagazine.com` is a **404** |
| Save.com | <https://www.save.com/manage> | Ex-RedPlum / RetailMeNot Everyday, now RR Donnelley / Valassis. Up to 6 weeks. `/delivery-options` redirects here |
| Valpak | <https://www.valpak.com/privacy-center> | The old `/remove-address` is **dead**. What's left is a data-erasure form with no mail-removal option — state the request in its free-text field, or email `privacy@valpak.com` |
| Money Mailer | via Catalog Choice (step 4) | No working self-serve form. Their own site lists `ListManagerContact@moneymailer.com` — note the junk-mail blogs circulate a **transposed, wrong** version of this address |

`Done: __________`

### 4. Catalogs — free, **ongoing**
**<https://www.catalogchoice.org>** (free account required)

~9,000–10,000 titles, and it submits the request on your behalf. This is the long
tail that step 2 structurally cannot reach — including charities and card issuers.

**This one is a habit, not a checkbox.** New catalog arrives → look it up → submit.

### 5. Upstream, so lists don't regrow — free, 5 years
**<https://www.experian.com/privacy/opting_out_direct_mail>**
(or 1-833-210-4615 / `optout@experian.com`)

The one big data broker that still runs a genuinely mail-specific opt-out. Acxiom,
Epsilon, LiveRamp and Data Axle have folded theirs into generic "do not sell"
forms; Oracle's is gone entirely.

`Done: __________  Renew by: __________ (5 years)`

### 6. California residents only — free
**<https://consumer.drop.privacy.ca.gov>**

One request forces 600+ registered data brokers to delete your data and stop
selling it; they must re-check every 45 days. Consumers could file from
2026-01-01 and **brokers became legally obligated to process on 2026-08-01**.
The strongest new lever in decades, but residency-gated.

`Done: __________`

### 7. Whatever still arrives — per company, ongoing
After steps 1–6 the residue is mail from organisations you have an existing
relationship with, which every centralised program exempts by design.

**There is no pattern to find.** Of eight large mailers checked on their own
websites, only **two** had a public web form; the rest were email, phone, an
account login, or nothing at all. So expect to send an email or make a call.

Practical tips:
- Keep the mailing label — several companies need the customer or catalog code
  printed on it (Lands' End asks for the "C number").
- Ask for two things at once: stop mailing me, **and** don't rent, sell or
  exchange my address. The second is what stops the mail reappearing under a
  different sender.
- **Charities**: there is no sector-wide do-not-solicit list, and once you've
  donated you're exempt from DMAchoice. Contact the organisation directly and
  enclose the mailing label — that's the BBB Wise Giving Alliance's own advice.
- **Last resort**: return the Business Reply Envelope with the label and a
  "remove me" note. The sender pays for it (USPS DMM 505 §1.3.1 guarantees they
  pay even for "incomplete, blank, or empty" returns). Legitimate, but its
  effectiveness is unproven — BREs often route to a fulfilment vendor rather than
  the list-hygiene team.

## Three things you'll read that are wrong

- **"Write REFUSED and put it back in the mailbox."** For unendorsed Marketing
  Mail, USPS simply discards it locally (DMM 507 Exhibit 1.5.3). The sender never
  finds out.
- **"Click Do Not Sell My Personal Information."** That governs *sharing data with
  third parties*. It does not stop a company mailing you from its own customer
  file — which is exactly the mail that survives steps 1–6.
- **"DeleteMe / Incogni / Optery will stop junk mail."** Their own pricing pages
  make **no** postal-mail claim; they sell people-search and data-broker removal.
  They rank for junk-mail searches purely through blog SEO.

Related: be sceptical of any phone number or address for an opt-out that you found
on a blog rather than the company's own site. Fabricated and transposed contact
details are common in that genre.

## What cannot be stopped

- **EDDM / "Postal Customer" saturation mail** — delivered to every address on a
  carrier route with no mailing list, so there is no name to suppress. (USPS does
  let an individual *mailer* honour a do-not-deliver address, but that's the
  sender's choice, not a lever you control.)
- **Political mail** — comes off state voter files and is explicitly exempt from
  DMAchoice. No nationwide mechanism exists.
- **First-party mail** from anyone you've bought from or donated to in the last
  two years — exempt from every centralised program. Step 7 is the only route.
- **Informed Delivery's own advertiser campaigns** — USPS offers no setting to
  suppress them; you can only switch the whole digest off. Stripping them, which
  is what this project does, is the only remedy that exists.

## The two things nobody mentions

1. **These expire.** OptOutPrescreen 5 years (unless permanent), DMAchoice 10,
   Experian 5.
2. **Moving silently undoes them.** They're keyed to your name *and* address, and
   a USPS change-of-address propagates through the industry's NCOA licensing —
   re-exposing your new address. Experian says outright that a move voids yours.
   **After any move, redo steps 1, 3 and 5.** The same applies if mail starts
   arriving under a name variant.
